Shooting & chance quality

Everything built on the expected-goals model from American Soccer Analysis (ASA).
xGExpected goals

The number of goals an average player would be expected to score from the same shots.

Every shot is assigned a scoring probability from its characteristics — distance, angle, body part, assist type, and the pattern of play. A player's xG is the sum of those probabilities. A shot worth 0.25 xG would be scored roughly one time in four.

xGF / xGAExpected goals for / against

Team-level xG created (for) and conceded (against).

Sum the xG of all shots a team takes (xGF) and all shots it faces (xGA). On this site both are shown per game, so teams that have played a different number of matches stay comparable.

xGDExpected goal difference

xGF minus xGA — the single best one-number summary of team quality.

xGF/game − xGA/game. Positive means a team creates better chances than it concedes. It predicts future results better than actual goal difference because it is less distorted by finishing luck and small samples.

G − xGGoals minus expected goals

Finishing performance relative to chance quality.

Actual goals scored minus xG. Positive means a player has out-shot their chances (good finishing, or luck). It is noisy over a single season — treat it as description, not prediction.

xAExpected assists

The xG of the shots a player's passes created.

For each pass that leads directly to a shot, take the xG of that shot and credit it to the passer. Rewards chance creation regardless of whether the teammate finished.

xPointsExpected points

League points a team 'deserved' from its chances.

For each match, the xG of both sides is turned into win/draw/loss probabilities, which convert to an expected points haul (3 × P(win) + 1 × P(draw)). Summed across matches. A team far below its xPoints has been unlucky, or is finishing poorly.

Player value

Metrics that value every action, not just shots.
g+Goals added

ASA's all-actions value metric: how much each touch changed the team's chance of scoring.

Every action (pass, dribble, shot, tackle, etc.) moves the ball into a new game state. g+ measures the change in the team's expected goal difference from before to after the action, credited to the player. Summed over a season and split by action type — Passing, Receiving, Dribbling, Shooting, Interrupting, Fouling.

Caveat. On this site g+ is shown 'above average', i.e. relative to a typical player's output for the same actions.

g+/96Goals added per 96 minutes

g+ scaled to a full match, so starters and substitutes compare fairly.

g+ ÷ minutes played × 96. Used to pick the 'key players' in each Inbox match preview.

Offensive / defensive g+

The same g+ total, split into what a player added on the ball and off it.

Offensive g+ sums the Passing, Receiving, Dribbling and Shooting actions; defensive g+ sums Interrupting and Fouling. The two add up to the total g+ shown in the squad table on each team page.

Caveat. Keepers are graded on the separate goalkeeper g+ model (Shotstopping, Handling, Claiming, Sweeping, Fielding, Distribution), which has no attacking/defending split, so they show a total only — and one that is not comparable to an outfielder's.

Per 96

Any rate stat scaled to one full match.

stat ÷ minutes played × 96. Ninety-six rather than ninety accounts for typical stoppage time. Rate stats are how you compare a player with 600 minutes to one with 2,400.

Percentile

How a player ranks against their own position group this season.

Each per-96 rate is ranked within the same general position (GK, CB, FB, DM, CM, AM, W, ST) among players clearing a 600-minute threshold. The 90th percentile means better than 90% of that position group. Forwards are never ranked against centre-backs.

Team tactics

Style axes derived from ASA passing and action-volume data. Each is scored 0–100 relative to the rest of the league this season.
Possession

Share of the passing in a team's own matches.

Own pass attempts ÷ (own + opponent pass attempts) across that team's games. A pass-share proxy for possession, since raw possession time is not in the free data.

Directness

How much ground a team's passes gain upfield.

Average vertical distance gained per pass, in yards. High directness means a team plays forward quickly; low means patient sideways/backwards circulation.

Press intensity (PPDA-style)Passes allowed per defensive action

How aggressively a team disrupts the opponent. Lower value = harder press.

Opponent pass attempts ÷ the team's own defensive actions (interruptions + fouls). Displayed as a 0–100 score where higher = presses harder.

Caveat. Conventional PPDA counts only actions in the attacking 60% of the pitch. The free ASA data has no pitch zones, so this is a whole-pitch approximation — a press proxy, not literal Opta PPDA.

Shot aggression

How readily a team turns possession into shots.

Shots taken per 100 of the team's own pass attempts. High means a team shoots early rather than working for a cleaner look.

Defensive solidity

Quality of chances conceded.

xG against per game, inverted so a higher score is a better defence.

Formation

The shape a team lines up in, e.g. 4-2-3-1.

Taken directly from ESPN's published lineup for each match. Team pages show the most-used shape for the season plus the full mix, since most sides vary it.

Caveat. Only available for seasons where ESPN match lineups have been ingested; older seasons show no formation.

Common XI

The eleven players with the most starts, drawn at their usual position.

Counts starts per player across the season and places each at their most frequent position slot. The number in each circle is their start count.

Caveat. A usage picture, not a specific lineup — those eleven may never have started a match together.

Style edge

Whether a tactical matchup historically favours one style, after removing team quality.

Every past match gets an expected xG difference from both teams' season xG rates plus home advantage. The residual — result minus expectation — is averaged for each style pairing. Because quality is already priced in, what's left is attributable to the matchup rather than to one side simply being better.

Caveat. Measured effects in the NWSL are small: across 1,488 matches only shot aggression and defensive solidity show any statistically distinguishable signal, and both explain well under 1% of variance. Treat every pairing with a small sample as noise, and note this is association, not causation. It is deliberately NOT fed into the match forecast — the strength model is already fit on results, so applying it again would double-count.

Counter-attack index

The signature of a team that cedes the ball and attacks vertically.

Directness percentile × (1 − possession percentile), scaled to 0–100. A team scores highly only when it is both direct and low-possession.

Simulation & forecasting

How the match and season projections are produced and checked.
Dixon-Coles

The match model — a bivariate Poisson with a low-score correction.

Each team gets an attack and a defence rating; goals are modelled as Poisson draws from those ratings plus a home-advantage term. The Dixon-Coles correction adjusts 0-0, 1-0, 0-1 and 1-1, which plain Poisson gets wrong. Ratings are fit on match results weighted by recency (180-day half-life) and blended with season xG to cut finishing noise.

Expected lineup

The XI a team is most likely to field — and who is missing.

Built from the last six matchday squads, weighting recent matches most and starts more than bench appearances. The 11 highest-scoring available players form the expected XI; players who have dropped out of squads appear under 'not featuring recently'.

Caveat. No free NWSL injury feed exists (ESPN's injury endpoint returns nothing for this league), so this reflects observed availability — injury, suspension, international duty, or simply being out of favour — not a medical report.

Lineup effect

How much the expected XI moves the forecast.

The expected XI's average g+/96 is compared to the team's whole recent squad. The gap shifts that team's attack and defence ratings (capped, to avoid overreacting to thin data). 'Lineup effect: Portland +1.5pp' means the adjustment raised Portland's win probability by 1.5 percentage points.

Monte Carlo

Playing out the rest of the season 10,000 times.

Every remaining fixture is simulated from its score matrix, the table is rebuilt, and the playoff bracket is played out. Doing that 10,000 times turns into the playoff / Shield / championship percentages.

Brier score

Forecast accuracy — lower is better.

Mean squared error between predicted probabilities and what happened, across win/draw/loss. A perfect forecast scores 0, and always predicting a flat 1/3 each scores 0.667. The site reports it against a naive baseline that knows only home-field advantage, so you can see whether the model actually adds anything.

Frozen forecast

The prediction the Results page scores — recorded before kickoff and never rewritten.

Each time the pipeline runs it stores its forecast for every upcoming fixture. While kickoff is still in the future the stored row is refreshed (later runs know more about lineups); the moment kickoff passes it is frozen. Matches are then scored against that locked-in prediction.

Caveat. Because forecasts can only be captured before a match, games played before this archive existed show no comparison rather than a back-filled one. Back-filling would score the model on hindsight and make the accuracy figures meaningless.

Match xG

The chance quality both teams actually created in a completed game.

ASA's per-match xG totals. On the Results page it sits between the pre-match projection and the goals actually scored, which separates three different things: what the model expected, what the teams actually created, and what the finishing produced.

Caveat. ASA publishes match xG on a 1–2 day lag, so the newest games may show it as pending rather than guessing a value.

BTTS / Over 2.5

Common match markets read off the score matrix.

The simulation produces a probability for every scoreline. Summing the relevant cells gives 'both teams to score' and 'total goals over 2.5'.

Market tracking

Terms used by the paper-trading scoreboard. Paper only — no real money.
De-vigRemoving the bookmaker margin

Converting posted prices into probabilities that sum to 100%.

Raw implied probabilities (1 ÷ decimal odds) sum to more than 1 — the excess is the bookmaker's margin. Dividing each by the total renormalises them to a fair probability set, which is what the model is compared against.

Edge

How much the model disagrees with the market.

Model probability − de-vigged market probability. A paper bet is logged when the edge clears 5 percentage points.

CLVClosing line value

Whether you got a better price than the market's final one.

Closing fair probability − entry fair probability. Consistently positive CLV is the strongest evidence a model is genuinely beating the market, and it shows up long before profit does.

ROIReturn on investment

Profit as a share of everything staked.

Total P&L ÷ total staked. Bets are settled at de-vigged fair odds, so this measures model skill, not a claim to beat the vig.
